# Answer-first arc

Use when the audience is a repeat, time-constrained decision-maker and the number *is* the deliverable.
They want the answer, then enough support to trust it. Do not make them scroll through method to reach the
verdict.

## Shape

1. **Verdict** (1 beat) — the answer, stated flat, in the first beat. The one number or recommendation the
   deck exists to deliver.
2. **What it rests on** (2-4 beats) — the two or three load-bearing facts, each its own beat, each with its
   source and grade visible. Not the full method, only the pillars the verdict stands on.
3. **One proof** (2-3 beats) — a single grounded worked example that shows the method is real, for the
   reader who wants to check. Concrete, named, one map scene per step.
4. **Bounds and caveats** (1-2 beats) — where the number is soft, what would change it, the honest edge.
5. **Restate + next step** (1 beat) — the verdict again, verbatim, plus the decision it enables.

## Beat skeleton

```
verdict    the answer, stated in beat one
pillar-01  load-bearing fact 1 + source/grade
pillar-02  load-bearing fact 2 + source/grade
pillar-03  load-bearing fact 3 + source/grade
proof-01   one named example, step 1
proof-02   one named example, step 2
bounds     where the number is soft
restate    the verdict again + the decision it enables
```

## Why the mirror still matters

Even answer-first restates. The verdict in beat one and the restate at the end are the same token. The
difference from build-to-verdict is only *when* the reader first sees the number, not whether it recurs.
