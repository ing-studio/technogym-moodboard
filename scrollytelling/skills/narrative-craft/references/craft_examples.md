# Craft examples

Neutral domain throughout (a hospital network), so the patterns read as craft, not as one vertical's
house style. Numbers are illustrative.

## Pivot-word worked example

Bad: "Wait times at emergency departments have been *steadily* increasing across the network, which is
concerning for outcomes and also suggests staffing may need review."
Three claims stacked (it rose, it is concerning, staffing may need review), and the pivot "steadily" is
vague. This explains, justifies, and implies in one sentence.

Good: "Emergency wait rose *quietly*: 41 minutes to 58, with no single spike."
One claim. "Quietly" passes **deletion** (remove it and the number still reads, but the point that it crept
up gradually rather than from one bad week is lost) and **uniqueness** (a neighbouring beat about one bad
shift would use a different pivot; this beat is precisely about the *absence* of a spike).

## Good/bad copy pairs

| Bad | Why | Good |
|---|---|---|
| "Let's take a look at how demand flows into the site." | throat-clearing opener | "Demand arrives from *three* directions." |
| "Volumes were seen to increase — notably in the north." | passive + em dash | "The north drove the rise: up 22%." |
| "This represents a significant and meaningful improvement." | hedge stack, no number | "Wait fell 17 minutes." |
| "Is this the whole story? Not quite." | rhetorical question | "One factor is missing: transfers." |
| "A holistic view lets us unpack the key drivers." | AI tells | "Two drivers explain most of it." |
| "Beds rose and also staffing rose and also acuity rose." | X and also Y and also Z | "Beds, staffing, acuity: all three rose." |

## Justification belongs in a .note

Bad (justification stacked on the claim):
> "Capture is 32% because the site sits 8 minutes from the nearest rival and online substitution is low."

Good (claim clean, justification demoted):
> Headline: "The site keeps *32%* of nearby demand."
> `.note`: "8 minutes to the nearest rival; online substitution is low for this category."

The claim sentence states one thing. The reason lives in the aside, where a reader who wants it can find it
and a reader who does not is not slowed.

## Structural worked example A — a regional network deck (many units)

A market-tour deck across several regions. Shape: hook (the network-wide headline) → tour (one beat per
region, each a profile plus a stat row, then a ranking beat that closes the tour) → pivot ("how we measure
demand") → method (drill into ONE named hospital: map the catchment, fence it, observe arrivals, trace
where patients come from, add the outcome layer) → aggregation (roll one hospital up to the region until
the number equals the hook) → close (mirror the intro). The signature move: the hook chips and the final
aggregation chips are identical.

## Structural worked example B — a single-unit deck (one place, deep)

The same spine applied to one hospital's emergency department. State the plan up front (a target: minutes
of wait, beds per thousand), prove it by showing the wider system it was learned from (all EDs light up →
cluster → a few exemplars → the archetypes), learn the signals, show one or two grounded patient-journey
proofs, then land a benchmark that says the target is reasonable. Same spine: state the plan, prove it from
the population it was learned on, close on the benchmark that makes it credible.
