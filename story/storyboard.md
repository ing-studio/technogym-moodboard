**Core message:** a mood cannot be built from single rooms. It is built from every layer inside a room and
from the top view of the whole building. Read that way, the plan asks for natural materials, and the images
in 03-mood form one idea: a timber spine with stone, earth, plaster and textile around it.

## Copy: 02 Floor plan

**plan-overview**
- Lede: All 6 floors at once. Each has a job, and the stairs join them, so a single visit crosses several floors.
- R Recover: the roof deck. T Train: the upper floors. S Studio: a group studio upstairs, cycling and boxing below. C Change: at the bottom and on the roof.
- Note: A room-by-room styling cannot see this. Level numbers and zones are our reading of the drawings, still to be confirmed.
- Bridge: On the way, the light changes.

**plan-light**
- Lede: 4 floors look out through long glazed facades. 2 have no windows at all. A finish chosen from a sunlit render will not survive the change.
- The plan asks the mood to: 1 work in daylight and without it; 2 stay calm on a full training floor; 3 run unbroken along the route, floor to floor; 4 care for changing and recovery as much as for training.
- Landing: One family of natural materials does all four: wood, stone, earth, plaster and textile.

## Copy: 03 Mood

**mood-natural**: each requirement answered by natural materials (daylight, density, continuity, coverage). Note: these materials are not decoration. Bridge: We bring them together in one idea.

**mood-spine**: one oak core from the lower ground to the roof; each floor adds the material its job needs. Core: oak and travertine, bronze at the stairs. Roof: oak, lime plaster, linen. Training floors: oak with steel. Studios: oak with rammed earth, low light. Lower ground: oak lockers, clay tile, warm cove light. Bridge: Material by material, here is what each one does.

**Material beats** (each: lede, Works, Looks, Where)
- Wood: oak for core, lockers, storage walls; walnut and figured veneer where members slow down. Works: takes knocks, refinished not replaced, slatted ceilings soften sound. Looks: warm in daylight and under lamps. Where: stair core, storage walls, lockers, studio ceilings.
- Stone and earth: travertine and rough stone at thresholds and wet areas; rammed earth and clay tile for weight and colour. Works: stone and glazed clay take water and wear; rammed earth needs no paint. Looks: earth tones warm windowless floors; one accent, terracotta red. Where: arrival and stairs, changing and showers, lower-ground walls.
- Lime plaster and warm light: plaster on ceilings, columns, curved walls, lit warm and low. Works: repairable, breathable, spreads light evenly. Looks: glow instead of glare. Where: studios, lounges, lower-ground ceilings, roof.
- Textile, leather and bronze: linen, bouclé, leather where bodies rest; bronze, brass, copper for details hands touch. Works: textile softens sound; leather and bronze wear in. Looks: a tactile layer for changing and recovery. Where: lounges, benches, changing rooms, fittings.

**mood-close**: the list of proposed natural materials: Wood (oak, walnut, figured veneer); Stone (travertine, rough natural stone); Earth (rammed earth, terracotta, glazed clay tile); Plaster (lime, textured plaster); Textile and leather (linen, bouclé, leather); Metal details (bronze, brass, copper). Landing: That is the difference between a styling and a mood we can build.
