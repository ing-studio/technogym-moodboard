Gym design, a mood board method

A mood is built from the whole building

A mood board is often a set of beautiful rooms. We build ours the other way round: from what was proposed, through everything it has to answer, down to the plan of every floor.

We start with the proposal.

What is proposed

A styling made to present the equipment

Technogym proposes a generic styling to show the equipment line at its best: each image is a single ideal room, seen at eye level, in a different setting.

What we have

Generic warm spaces, well lit, natural materials.

What it offers

A calm, warm direction: sand-coloured machines, natural timber, soft daylight.

It shows rooms. A gym is a building, so we need to look further.

What we need to look at

Read it by material

Strip the render back and it is a handful of materials. Each one carries a meaning the mood can keep, change or replace.

  1. 1Oak: warmth and craft. It makes a gym feel lived in rather than engineered.
  2. 2Glass: openness and daylight, but also exposure. It only means something where there is a view.
  3. 3Sand-coloured metal: calm equipment that blends into the room instead of dominating it.
  4. 4Steel and textile: strength beside softness, the weight you lift and the rug you stretch on.

Only the equipment finish comes from Technogym. Every other material is ours to choose, and each choice sets the mood.

Materials give a room its meaning. To see how the rooms work together, we look from above.

What needs to be assessed

Three points to assess before we build the mood

Look past the materials and the renders raise three questions the building has to answer. None of them rules the styling out.

  1. 1Daylight
    Daylight here means the comfort of natural light, soft and ambient, filling the room evenly, layered with targeted spot lighting that picks out the body in motion and brings out its relief and definition.
  2. 2Density
    The space is kept deliberately spare, its visual density low, so nothing competes with the person using it. It is never overdesigned: the room recedes, the equipment and the body stay the focus, and the interior reads as built around the client rather than around itself.
  3. 3Continuity
    One building, one body is the core idea: the space and the person using it are conceived together, not as separate problems. Style, materials, and approach carry through the entire structure without interruption, so that no room feels like a departure from the last. The building reads as a single, continuous thought from entrance to equipment to body.

The mood we suggest answers all three together: every material, every floor.

That is why the plan comes before the mood.

Top down

One building, one body

Seen from above, the ? floors are not a stack of separate rooms. They work as one organism.

The stairs are its spine, the corridors its circulation, the facades the places where it takes in light. Members move through it the way blood moves through a body: down to change, up to train, back again to recover.

No floor works on its own, so no floor can have a mood of its own. The mood has to run through the whole body, in daylight and without it.

Level numbers are our reading of the drawings, still to be confirmed.

That asks for one family of materials.

Why natural materials

Why the mood is built from natural materials

The building asks the mood to work in daylight and without it, on full floors, and along the whole route. Every answer points to materials that grew, or came out of the ground.

  1. 1Daylight: wood, stone and plaster stay warm under artificial light, so the lower floors feel as calm as the sunlit ones.
  2. 2Density: timber battens and textile soften the acoustic noise of a full floor, while oak storage walls keep equipment off it. What matters is getting the density right: the right materials, in the right amount, so the effect stays calm rather than tipping into clutter or excess.
  3. 3Continuity: one family of natural materials on every floor makes the building read as one place.

These materials are not decoration. Without them, each of the three points needs a separate fix.

We bring them together in one idea.

Our proposal

Wood at the heart, warmth everywhere

Wood is the heart of the mood. It brings warmth, touch and a sense of craft that no other material gives in quite the same way, so it stays, throughout the building.

A building made only of wood would feel heavy. So we let its warmth travel into other materials that carry the same feeling with a lighter hand.

  • Wood: the constant, the material everything else answers to.
  • Plaster and stucco: the warmth of wood turned into surface and soft light.
  • Textile: the warmth of wood turned into softness and quiet.

Material by material, here is what each one does.

Material: wood

Wood carries the spine

Oak for the stair core, lockers and storage walls. Walnut where members slow down, and cork veneer where hands and bodies meet the wall.

  • Works: oak takes daily knocks and is sanded and refinished instead of replaced. Cork veneer is warm to the touch, gives slightly under pressure and absorbs sound, so studios and stretching areas stay quiet.
  • Looks: warm in daylight and under lamps, so upper and lower floors feel like one building. Cork adds a pale, living texture beside the smooth oak.
  • Where: stair core, storage walls, lockers, studio ceilings; cork on studio and stretching-zone walls.

Material: stone and earth

Stone and earth ground it

Travertine, terrazzo and rough stone at thresholds and in wet areas. Rammed earth where walls need weight and colour.

  • Works: travertine and terrazzo take water and wear in showers and entrances; rammed earth walls are solid and need no paint.
  • Looks: layered earth tones warm the floors without daylight and give the building one accent: terracotta red.
  • Where: arrival and stairs, changing rooms and showers, lower-ground walls.

Material: lime plaster and light

Plaster holds the light

Lime and textured plaster on ceilings, columns and curved walls, lit warm and low.

  • Works: repairable, breathable surfaces that spread light evenly, so windowless floors do not feel closed.
  • Looks: a soft, hand-made texture that turns lamps into glow instead of glare.
  • Where: studios, lounges, lower-ground ceilings and the roof.

Beyond plaster: the LED screen room

A wall of screens holds light the way plaster does, wrapping the whole room in it. But it can also change: a sky over a floor with no windows, a slow landscape for recovery, a bright scene for a class. One surface, many moods.

Material: textile, leather and copper

Textile and leather make it soft

Linen, bouclé and leather wherever the body slows down: on benches, in lounges and in the quiet corners between training and changing. A fine copper screen divides space without closing it.

After the effort of a workout, the body looks for comfort. Hard surfaces carry the energy of the training floor; soft ones signal that it is time to rest. Textile is how the building tells the body to breathe out.

  • Works: upholstery, curtains and textile panels absorb sound, so lounges and changing areas stay calm even when the floors above are full. Leather is hard-wearing and easy to clean, and ages gracefully with daily use.
  • Looks: a tactile layer of soft weaves and warm leather that makes changing and recovery feel as considered as training. Copper mesh adds a glow of light and a sense of privacy without heaviness.
  • Where: lounges, benches, changing rooms and studio thresholds, where members pause, sit and reset between one part of the visit and the next.

The materials we propose

A mood built from the whole building

Read from the top view, tested on every floor, made of natural materials that work in any light.

  • Wood: oak, walnut, cork and figured veneer
  • Stone: travertine, terrazzo and rough natural stone
  • Earth: rammed earth, terracotta
  • Plaster: lime and textured plaster
  • Textile and leather: linen, bouclé, leather
  • Metal details: bronze, brass, copper

That is the difference between a styling and a mood we can build.

Bright accents

Calm materials, a few bright moments

Against a quiet base of wood, stone, plaster and textile, a few bright accents give the building energy. They are placed deliberately, where the body moves and where the eye needs a signal.

  • LED displays: screens and light panels change colour and scene, a forest for a yoga class, a vivid glow for a high-intensity session. The room changes its mood without changing its materials.
  • Billboards and posters: large, soft-focus images of movement bring motivation to the walls, like a poster in a stadium corridor, without cluttering the space.
  • The bright vertical connection: the staircase is the spine of the building, so it becomes its brightest element. A strong colour pulls members up and down between floors and makes the route easy to read.

Accents stay few and concentrated. Colour appears in a small number of places, so the calm of the natural materials holds everywhere else.
