Gym design, a mood board method

A mood is built from the whole building

A mood board is often a set of beautiful rooms. We build ours the other way round: from what was proposed, through everything it has to answer, down to the plan of every floor.

What is proposed

A styling made to present the equipment

Technogym proposes a generic styling. Its purpose is to show the equipment line at its best, so each image is a single ideal room, seen at eye level, in a different setting.

It shows rooms. A gym is a building, so we need to look further.

What we need to look at

Read it in layers

A render shows one room at eye level. Every picture holds four layers, and each one is a separate decision.

  1. 1Shell: ceiling, walls and floor
  2. 2Light: daylight and artificial light
  3. 3Equipment: the Technogym line
  4. 4Objects: storage, rugs, art and planting

Only layer 3 comes from Technogym. Everything else is designed for this building.

Layers explain a room. To see how the rooms work together, we look from above.

What needs to be assessed

Four points to assess before we build the mood

From above, three more aspects appear: zones, route and depth. Read together with the layers, they raise four points. None of them rules the styling out.

  1. 1DaylightLight, depth
    Every render relies on large windows and a view. Deep and lower-ground floors have neither, so the mood cannot depend on daylight.
  2. 2DensityEquipment, zones
    The renders show a few machines in open space. The plan places rows of stations, so the calm has to survive a full floor.
  3. 3ContinuityRoute
    Each render is a separate room in a different place. Members move through one sequence, so the mood has to hold from the entrance to the last studio.
  4. 4CoverageZones, route
    Only training is shown. Arrival, changing rooms and circulation are on the plan and still need a styling.

The mood we suggest answers all four together: every layer, every zone, every floor.

That is why the plan comes before the mood.

What each floor is for

Every floor has a job

Sorted by use, the floors fall into four kinds of space.

  1. RRecover: the roof, an open deck with loungers.
  2. TTrain: the upper floors, with cardio along the windows and strength deeper in.
  3. SStudio: a group studio upstairs, a cycling room and a boxing ring below ground.
  4. CChange: at the bottom and on the roof, with a locker hall, private cabins and a lounge by the stair.

Zones are our reading of the drawings.

A single visit uses several of them.

How members move

One visit, several floors

Changing sits at the bottom and at the top, training and studios in between. Every visit climbs or descends through the same stairs, so the route is where the mood must never break.

A room-by-room styling cannot see this. Only the top view shows it.

On the way, the light changes.

What the plan asks

The same mood, with and without daylight

? floors look out through long glazed facades. ? have no windows at all. A finish chosen from a sunlit render will not survive the change.

The plan asks the mood to

  1. 1Work in daylight and without it
  2. 2Stay calm on a full training floor
  3. 3Run unbroken along the route, floor to floor
  4. 4Care for changing and recovery as much as for training

One material family does all four: natural wood, with stone and plaster.

* Level names are still to be confirmed.

The mood chapter shows why, and how.

Why natural materials

Why the mood starts with wood

Each requirement from the plan points to the same material.

  1. 1Daylight: oak, stone and plaster stay warm under artificial light, so the lower floors feel as calm as the sunlit ones.
  2. 2Density: timber slats over acoustic backing soften a full floor, and oak storage walls keep equipment off it.
  3. 3Continuity: one family of oak, stone and plaster on every floor makes the building read as one place.
  4. 4Coverage: lockers, benches and lounges in the same wood give changing and recovery the care training gets.

Wood is not decoration here. Without it, each of the four points needs a separate fix.

So we give timber a structure.

Our proposal

A timber spine through every floor

One oak core runs from the lower ground to the roof. Every floor grows from it and adds one companion material for its job.

  • Core: oak meeting travertine, with bronze details at every stair.
  • Roof: oak with plaster and linen, open and light for recovery.
  • Training floors: oak with steel, built for heavy daily use.
  • Studios: oak with stone and focused, low light.
  • Lower ground: oak lockers and warm cove light where daylight never reaches.

It has to work as hard as it looks.

Functional and beautiful

Built to work, made to be seen

The mood is not a layer added at the end. Each timber element solves something the plan asks for.

  • Stores: an oak wall of pegs keeps weights off the floor and on display.
  • Quiets: slatted ceilings over acoustic backing take the edge off a busy floor.
  • Lasts: solid oak with rounded edges is sanded and refinished, not replaced.
  • Guides: lit timber shelving and warm light lines lead members from the stair to the floor.

Put together, this is the mood we suggest.
